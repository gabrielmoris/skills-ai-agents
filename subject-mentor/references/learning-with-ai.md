# Learning with AI without losing the skill

## Research

- Bastani et al., "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning", PNAS 122(26), 2025 (~1,000 high-school students). Unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice performance but lowered unassisted exam scores by ~17% vs. control. A tutor version with guardrails (no direct answers, hints) largely removed the harm.
- Shen & Tamkin (Anthropic), "How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills", Jan 2026 (RCT, 52 developers learning the Trio async library). AI group scored ~17% lower on a comprehension quiz, biggest gap in debugging, with no significant speed gain. Learners who used AI for conceptual questions kept their learning; those who delegated code generation lost it.
- Jošt, Taneski & Karakatič, Applied Sciences 2024 (students learning React): LLM use for code generation/debugging correlated negatively with grades; use for explanations did not.
- Buçinca et al. 2021: "cognitive forcing functions" (make the human commit to a judgment before seeing AI output) reduce over-reliance.

## Rules

1. **Learning mode vs. delivery mode.** Decide which one you are in. In learning mode, AI explains and tests; you write the code. In delivery mode, delegate freely — in areas you already master.
2. **Commit first.** Write your answer, design, or hypothesis before asking AI. Then compare.
3. **Ask "why", not "write".** Conceptual questions preserve learning; delegation does not.
4. **Read every line you ship.** If you can't explain it, you don't merge it.
5. **Keep AI-free reps.** Regular closed-book practice (build-from-blank, debugging without AI) to measure true skill.
6. **Keep the decisions.** Let AI critique your architecture; don't let it make it — in areas where judgment is what you are building.
7. **Log it.** Keep a learning log and decision records; they are your retrieval material.
